Currency+ and Currency History: Rates, Charts, Zoom and Pan

Compare multiple currencies, calculate expressions, refresh rates every minute, explore exchange-rate history and learn the two-finger chart controls coming in 3.1.2.

By Adysseus, publisher of Conversion Calculator Plus

Currency+ brings several currency comparisons into one list. Currency History adds context: a chart of how the selected exchange rate has changed over time. Use them together to compare an amount today, inspect earlier readings and keep previously loaded data available when you lose your connection.

Chart gestures are coming in version 3.1.2.
The public App Store version was 3.1.1 when this guide was updated on 22 October 2026. The pinch, two-finger pan and reset instructions below describe the upcoming 3.1.2 update.

Compare several currencies with one input

Open Currency+ and add the currencies you want to compare. A list might start with USD and include EUR, GBP, JPY and supported crypto such as BTC. Supported precious-metal quotes can also be added. Currency availability and quote coverage depend on the data available in the app.

Enter an amount in the active card and the other amounts update together. Tap a different amount card to make that currency your input, without rebuilding the list. The advanced calculator accepts arithmetic and brackets, so you can enter (12 × 3) + 10, calculate 46, and see the converted amounts directly. It is useful for totaling items before comparing their cost in several currencies.

Up-to-the-minute comparisons

Currency+ checks for updated rates every minute while its rates page is visible, the app is active and the device is connected. It also fetches rates when you enter the screen, and provides a manual refresh button. Look at the displayed update time to see when your data was last refreshed.

A one-minute refresh interval does not mean that every source publishes a different quote each minute. A refresh can return the same rate, and an unsuccessful update keeps the last accepted data. These are reference conversions; an actual bank or card transaction can use a different rate and include fees.

Open Currency History and choose a range

Tap the chart button beside a currency to open the selected pair’s history. Choose 1 day, 1 week, 1 month, 3 months, 1 year, 5 years or all available history. Short ranges help you inspect recent movement; longer ranges show a broader picture. The amount summary uses your conversion amount, so a historical reading has a useful monetary context.

The available observations and length of history vary by pair. “All” means the history available for that pair, not a promise of an unlimited record. The Min and Max labels summarize the loaded range. Currency History is separate from automatic conversion history, which records your earlier calculations.

Read a point with one finger

Tap or drag across the chart with one finger to select an actual recorded point. The readout shows its date or time, exchange rate and converted amount. In 3.1.2, this still works after you zoom or pan: one finger inspects readings, while two fingers move the chart.

Zoom and pan in 3.1.2

  1. Pinch to zoom. Spread two fingers to zoom in, or pinch them together to zoom out. Both the horizontal time axis and vertical exchange-rate axis scale around your fingers.
  2. Pan with two fingers. Once zoomed in, drag with two fingers to move left or right through time, or up and down through the rate range. Movement stays within the loaded chart bounds.
  3. Inspect with one finger. Tap or drag to select a reading without moving the chart.
  4. Double-tap to reset. Restore the full chart. Changing the currency pair or time range also resets the view.

Zooming changes the view, not the underlying rates. It does not fetch older observations beyond the loaded range, and the Min and Max summary continues to describe that loaded range.

Keep rates and charts available offline

Load the pairs and time ranges you need while connected. Previously loaded rates and charts can then be viewed offline. A new pair, an uncached range or a fresh quote still needs a connection. Saved rates retain their timestamp; going offline does not make them current.

On a wider iPad or an open iPhone Duo window, Currency+ can show its currency list beside the history chart, making it easier to compare an amount and inspect a rate together. For a trip, open the currencies and charts you expect to use before leaving Wi-Fi, and check their update times.
