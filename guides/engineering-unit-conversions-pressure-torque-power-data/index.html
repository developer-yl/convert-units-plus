Engineering Unit Conversions: Pressure, Torque, Power and Data

A practical guide to pressure in pascals, kilopascals and bar; torque versus energy; watts versus watt-hours; and decimal versus binary data units.

By Adysseus, publisher of Conversion Calculator Plus

Engineering notes, product specifications and dashboards often mix unit systems. A conversion calculator can translate the number, but a correct interpretation starts with the quantity being measured. Pressure is not torque, power is not energy, and a decimal gigabyte is not the same size as a gibibyte. Use the following examples as unit literacy, then confirm any design limits or operating requirements with the responsible technical source.

Pressure: Pa, kPa and bar

The pascal (Pa) is the SI unit of pressure and equals one newton per square meter. A kilopascal is 1,000 pascals, and one bar equals 100,000 pascals, or 100 kPa. These relationships make conversion straightforward:

  • 250 kPa × 1,000 = 250,000 Pa
  • 250 kPa ÷ 100 = 2.5 bar

When comparing gauges, reports or equipment labels, also check whether the value is absolute pressure or gauge pressure. Converting units does not resolve that distinction, and a numerical match does not establish that a component is suitable for a particular application.

Torque is a turning effect

Torque describes a rotational effect: a force applied at a distance from an axis. Its common SI unit is the newton meter (N·m). Energy and work also have the dimensions of force times distance, and one joule is one newton meter in unit terms. Yet torque and energy describe different quantities, so torque should be written as N·m rather than relabeled as joules. For example, a specification of 12 N·m describes torque; it does not mean that 12 J of work was necessarily performed. The result depends on how the force and rotation act in the actual system.

Power is a rate; watt-hours are energy

A watt (W) measures power, or energy transferred per unit time: 1 W = 1 J/s. A watt-hour (Wh) measures energy over time. For a simple arithmetic example, a device operating at a steady 60 W for half an hour uses 30 Wh, because 60 W × 0.5 h = 30 Wh. Since one hour is 3,600 seconds, 30 Wh is 108,000 J, or 108 kJ. Keep the quantity and time interval attached to the answer; a watt value alone is not an energy total.

Data: GB and GiB

Storage and data-transfer descriptions may use decimal SI prefixes or binary prefixes. One gigabyte (GB) is 1,000,000,000 bytes. One gibibyte (GiB) is 1,073,741,824 bytes, or 230 bytes. A GB is therefore smaller than a GiB. Similarly, MB means 1,000,000 bytes while MiB means 1,048,576 bytes. If two specifications appear to disagree, check whether one uses decimal units and the other binary units before assuming the capacity changed.

A practical workflow is to write down the quantity, preserve its unit symbol, convert to the requested unit, and check the magnitude against an estimate.
