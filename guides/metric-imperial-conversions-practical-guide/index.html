Metric and Imperial Conversions: A Practical Guide

Convert meters, feet, kilograms, pounds and temperatures with worked examples, and learn why a US gallon differs from an Imperial gallon.

By Adysseus, publisher of Conversion Calculator Plus

Metric and Imperial measurements appear side by side in travel, recipes, product dimensions and technical manuals. A reliable unit converter helps, but understanding the relationship between units makes the result easier to check. Start by identifying the quantity, keep the unit attached to every number, and use the correct conversion for the system named in the source.

1. Convert meters to feet

The international foot is defined as exactly 0.3048 meter. To convert meters to feet, divide by 0.3048, or multiply by about 3.28084. For example, 1.80 m ÷ 0.3048 = 5.9055 ft, so a height of 1.80 m is about 5 ft 11 in. The final rounded value is convenient for everyday reading; keep more digits during intermediate calculations if precision matters.

2. Convert kilograms to pounds

The avoirdupois pound is exactly 0.45359237 kg. To convert kilograms to pounds, divide by that factor, or multiply by about 2.20462. For example, 70 kg × 2.20462 = 154.3234 lb, commonly rounded to 154.3 lb. To reverse the conversion, multiply pounds by 0.45359237. Notice the direction: because a pound is less than a kilogram, the number of pounds is larger than the number of kilograms for the same mass.

3. Convert Celsius and Fahrenheit

Temperature scales use both an offset and a different step size, so do not convert them by multiplying alone. The formulas are:

  • °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32
  • °C = (°F − 32) × 5/9

For a worked example, 20 °C × 9/5 + 32 = 68 °F. To convert back, (68 − 32) × 5/9 = 20 °C. The 32-degree offset explains why 0 °C is not 0 °F.

4. Check which gallon a source means

“Gallon” is not one universal volume. A U.S. liquid gallon is about 3.785412 liters; an Imperial gallon used in the United Kingdom and some other contexts is 4.54609 liters. An Imperial gallon is about 20 percent larger. If a fuel listing or recipe simply says “gallon,” check its country or source before converting. For example, 10 U.S. gallons is about 37.85 L, while 10 Imperial gallons is 45.46 L. The difference is large enough to change a comparison.

5. Use a quick checking routine

  1. Name the quantity. Length, mass, temperature and volume need different rules.
  2. Confirm the exact unit. Check whether a source means U.S. customary or Imperial units.
  3. Set the direction. Decide which unit you have and which one you need.
  4. Estimate first. A meter is a little over three feet; a kilogram is a little over two pounds.
  5. Round at the end. Preserve useful digits in the calculation, then round for the task.

A metric and Imperial converter is especially useful when comparing travel distances, cooking volumes, package sizes or temperature settings.
